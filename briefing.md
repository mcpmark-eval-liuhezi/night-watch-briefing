# The Night Watch — Podcast Research Pack

Research pack for the upcoming episode on Rembrandt's *The Night Watch* (Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam). Everything below was pulled from live sources on the day of compilation — the Rijksmuseum collection services and live web pages — nothing filled in from memory.

---

## 1. Museum Record

Pulled from the Rijksmuseum collection services by inventory number.

- **Object number:** SK-C-5 — **confirmed.** The record resolved successfully under this inventory number.
- **Title, exactly as the museum's collection data records it:** *Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'*
  - The English-language collection page titles it: *The Night Watch — Militia Company of District II under the Command of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq*
- **Year the record dates it to:** 1642 (record date field: 1642-01-01)
- **Maker:** Rembrandt van Rijn (the collection page records the inscription *signature and date: 'Rembrandt f 1642'*; painted in Amsterdam)
- **Museum's permanent link for the work:** https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-C-5
  - Persistent record ID returned by the collection data: https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928
- Additional detail from the collection page: oil on canvas; height 379.5 cm x width 453.5 cm (weights also recorded: 337 kg and 170 kg); on display in the Night Watch Gallery.

## 4. Web Fact-Check

### Completion year

A Google search ("Night Watch Rembrandt 1642 completion year Operation Night Watch research project") returned results consistent with the museum record, all dating the painting to **1642**:
- France24 result: "The multi-million-euro restoration starting in July 2019 will be open to the two million people a year... Dutch master's 1642 tableau."
- Sotheby's result: "Commissioned in 1642 by the mayor and leader of the civic guard of Amsterdam, Frans Banninck Cocq, to create a group portrait of his shooting..."
- artrkl.com result: "'The Night Watch' (1642) is one of Rembrandt's most famous paintings depicting the Militia Company of District II in the Netherlands."

The museum's own collection page (opened directly) confirms 1642, including the painted signature "Rembrandt f 1642".

### Current angle: Operation Night Watch

**Source opened and read:** Rijksmuseum press release, "Restoration begins of Rembrandt's The Night Watch," publication date **12 November 2024** — https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/press/press-releases/restoration-begins-of-rembrandt-s-the-night-watch (supporting page: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/stories/operation-night-watch)

Concrete details from the page:
1. **12 November 2024** — following **five years of intensive research** and the re-stretching of the painting, a team of **eight Rijksmuseum conservators** started removing the varnish from The Night Watch, marking the beginning of the **second phase of Operation Night Watch**, "the biggest ever research and restoration project devoted to this masterpiece."
2. The conservators use a **special type of tissue prepared with a solvent**, placed against the surface for a short, limited amount of time; the varnish layers being removed were applied during the painting's restoration in **1975-1976**, and remnants of even older varnish are removed with cotton swabs under a microscope. The work is carried out with microscopic precision in a see-through glass chamber in the Night Watch Gallery, in view of the visiting public.
3. The research partners named are **AkzoNobel** (main partner), the Cultural Heritage Agency of the Netherlands (RCE), Delft University of Technology (TU Delft), the University of Amsterdam (UvA), Amsterdam University Medical Centre (AUMC), the University of Antwerp (UA), Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam (VU), Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e) and the National Gallery of Art, Washington DC. **Taco Dibbits, Director of the Rijksmuseum**, is quoted: "The start of the restoration phase is filled with anticipation: removing the varnish will expose the eventful history of The Night Watch..."

The museum's Operation Night Watch story page adds that the project — "the biggest and most wide-ranging ever study and restoration of Rembrandt's most famous painting" — started in summer 2019 and is being carried out in full view of the visiting public in a specially designed glass chamber.
